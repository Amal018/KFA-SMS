Title: Basic Art Supplies Every Beginner Needs (and What to Skip)
Date: 2026-10-10
Description: A simple, budget-friendly list of drawing and painting supplies for children and adult beginners — plus the expensive things you do not need yet.

---
Walk into any stationery shop in Coimbatore and the art aisle can be overwhelming. The good news: beginners need far less than you think. Here is a practical starter list.

## For drawing

- **Graphite pencils:** an HB, a 2B and a 4B or 6B are enough to learn shading.
- **A good eraser:** a soft white eraser plus a kneaded eraser for lifting highlights.
- **Sharpener:** one that does not break leads — or a craft knife for older students.
- **Paper:** a sketchbook with medium-weight paper (around 120–160 gsm).
- **Coloured pencils:** a 12- or 24-colour set is plenty for children.

## For painting

- **Watercolour:** a student-grade pan set, two or three round brushes (small, medium, large) and proper watercolour paper — ordinary paper buckles when wet.
- **Acrylic:** five tubes are enough to mix almost anything: white, black, a red, a yellow and a blue. Add flat and round brushes.
- **A palette:** a plastic palette or even an old ceramic plate.
- **Two water jars:** one for rinsing, one for clean water.
- **A cloth or tissue** for blotting brushes.

## What to skip for now

- Huge 48- or 72-colour sets — beginners learn more by mixing a few colours.
- Expensive artist-grade paints — student grade is fine while you learn.
- Easels and canvases — paper on a table works perfectly at the start.
- Specialist brushes you have never used.

## Look after your brushes

Rinse brushes straight after use, reshape the tips with your fingers and store them upright or flat — never standing on their bristles in water. Good care makes cheap brushes last for months.
