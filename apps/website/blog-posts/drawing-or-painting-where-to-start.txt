When people look for a "painting class near me", they are often surprised when an art teacher suggests starting with drawing. Here is why — and when it makes sense to jump straight into paint.

## Drawing is the foundation

Almost every painting starts as a drawing. Drawing teaches the three things every artist needs:

- **Observation** — really looking at shapes, edges and proportions
- **Light and shade** — understanding how form is created by tone
- **Composition** — deciding where things go on the page

With these skills in place, painting becomes far less frustrating. Without them, beginners often struggle with paintings that look flat or out of proportion, and blame the paint.

## But painting teaches things drawing cannot

Colour is its own language. Mixing paints, controlling water in watercolour, layering in acrylic and blending in oil are separate skills that take time. Painting also tends to feel more expressive and freeing, which keeps many learners motivated.

## A sensible order for children

1. **Ages 5–7:** pencil drawing and colouring — building control and colour sense together.
2. **Ages 8–12:** structured drawing fundamentals and shading.
3. **Ages 13 and above:** watercolour, oil and acrylic painting on top of strong drawing skills.

This is the progression we follow at Kalalaya, because each stage prepares for the next.

## For adults, start with what excites you

Adults are usually clear about why they want to learn. If you are drawn to a particular style — glass painting, mandala art, watercolour landscapes — start there. Enjoyment keeps you practising, and you can fill gaps in drawing skills along the way. Many adults later add a drawing course once they see how much it helps.

## The short answer

- Learning for the long term, especially children: **start with drawing**.
- Adult wanting a creative hobby quickly: **start with the medium you love**.
