Parents often ask us, "Is my child old enough for an art class?" The honest answer is that there is no single perfect age — but there is a right *kind* of class for each age. Here is what to expect at each stage.

## Ages 5–7: play with purpose

Around age five, most children can hold a pencil with control, follow simple instructions and sit for a short session. This is a great time to start. Classes at this age should feel like play while quietly building:

- Fine motor skills and pencil grip
- Recognising and naming colours and shapes
- Staying focused on one activity for longer
- Confidence to try without fear of "getting it wrong"

## Ages 8–12: the skill-building years

This is when structured drawing really takes off. Children can now understand proportion, light and shade, and simple perspective. A good class introduces fundamentals step by step — fruits and objects, symmetrical designs, 3D shapes, pencil shading and the basics of portraits. Progress is fast and very visible, which keeps motivation high.

## Ages 13–18: style and seriousness

Teenagers can work with more demanding mediums — watercolour, oil, acrylic and charcoal — and longer projects like landscapes, portraits and still life. For students considering design, architecture or fine-arts courses, these years are the time to build a portfolio. Some students also prepare for the Tamil Nadu Government Technical Examination in drawing and painting.

## Adults: it is never too late

Many of our students started as adults. Without exams or school pressure, adults often progress quickly because they practise by choice. Short-term courses — glass painting, mural work, mandala art — are a popular way to begin.

## Signs your child is ready

- They draw or colour on their own without being asked
- They can sit with one activity for 20–30 minutes
- They ask how to draw something "properly"
- They enjoy showing you what they made

## Starting late is not a problem

A child who starts at ten is not "behind". Drawing is a skill like any other — regular practice and good teaching matter far more than the age you begin.
